You are designing a high-fidelity UI prototype for a single section of a 
larger platform: the "Training & AI" module of a job-insertion platform 
aimed at recent high school graduates from vulnerable neighborhoods in 
Latin America. This is NOT the full platform — only this module.

---

## CONTEXT & PHILOSOPHY

The platform bridges employment and education. When a candidate applies 
for a job but lacks certain skills, they enter a training process before 
being able to officially apply. This module handles that entire training 
journey.

Target users:
- CANDIDATE (postulante): A young person (17–22 years old) from a 
  vulnerable area. May have low confidence. Needs clarity, motivation, 
  and simplicity.
- TUTOR: A human mentor who monitors candidates, receives alerts, and 
  can intervene when needed.

Design philosophy:
- Desktop-first web layout (minimum 1280px wide reference frame)
- Two-panel or three-column layouts where appropriate
- Warm, motivational, never punitive tone
- Progress must ALWAYS be visible
- Simple language, no technical jargon in UI labels
- Feels modern and approachable, NOT corporate or bureaucratic
- Think Duolingo meets LinkedIn Learning, but warmer
- Make full use of horizontal space — avoid single-column stacked 
  layouts that look like a stretched mobile screen

---

## DESIGN SYSTEM

Color palette:
- Primary: Indigo / deep blue (#4F46E5 or similar) — trust, education
- Accent: Amber / warm orange (#F59E0B or similar) — energy, action
- Success: Emerald green (#10B981)
- Neutral backgrounds: Zinc/slate scale (white, #F8FAFC, #F1F5F9)
- Text: Near-black (#1E293B), medium gray (#64748B)

Typography:
- Modern sans-serif (Inter or similar)
- Clear hierarchy: large bold headings, readable body text
- Avoid all-caps except for labels/tags

Components:
- Rounded corners (12–16px radius) throughout
- Soft shadows, not harsh borders
- Cards as the primary layout unit
- Progress bars and circular progress rings
- Status badges: Pending (gray), In Progress (blue), Completed (green), 
  Needs Attention (amber)
- Avatar placeholders for tutor presence
- Persistent left sidebar navigation for all views
- Top topbar with user info, notifications icon, and avatar

---

## GLOBAL LAYOUT

Every screen shares the same shell:
- LEFT SIDEBAR (fixed, ~240px wide): logo area at top, navigation links, 
  tutor presence card at the bottom for the candidate side
- TOP BAR (fixed, full width): page title on the left, notification bell 
  + user avatar on the right
- MAIN CONTENT AREA: fills the remaining space, scrollable

The sidebar for CANDIDATES contains:
  Home | My Plan | Training | Progress | My Tutor

The sidebar for TUTORS contains:
  My Candidates | Alerts | Reports

---

## SCREENS TO DESIGN

Design ALL of the following screens within the global layout shell 
described above.

---

### SCREEN 1 — CANDIDATE DASHBOARD (Home)
The first thing the candidate sees after logging in.

Layout: use a multi-column grid to fill horizontal space.

Must include:
- A wide welcome banner across the top of the content area with the 
  candidate's name and the job offer they applied to 
  (e.g., "Welcome back, Lucía — Training for: Junior Administrative 
  Assistant at Empresa X")
- Left column: a prominent OVERALL PROGRESS card with a circular ring 
  indicator (e.g., "47% complete") and a motivational subtitle 
  ("You're halfway there!")
- Center/right column: "Skill Gap" summary card showing which skills 
  were missing vs already covered, using colored tags or a 
  checklist-style layout
- A "Continue Training" CTA button — prominent, leads to current module
- Bottom row: "Your Tutor" card (tutor name, photo placeholder, status 
  indicator, "Send message" button) alongside a "Recent Activity" 
  mini-feed

---

### SCREEN 2 — TRAINING PLAN (My Plan)
A structured view of the full training path.

Layout: two columns — module list on the left (~40%), module detail 
preview on the right (~60%) when a module is selected.

Must include:
- Left panel: vertical step-by-step list of all training modules. Each 
  item shows module title, estimated duration, and status badge 
  (Completed / In Progress / Locked). Completed modules are ticked off, 
  locked ones are dimmed with a lock icon, current one is highlighted.
- Right panel: when a module is selected or hovered, show an expanded 
  detail card with module description, list of skills it covers, 
  estimated duration, and a "Start" or "Continue" CTA button.
- Top of content area: overall estimated completion time and a 
  horizontal progress bar spanning the full width
- Optional XP or point counter as a gamification hint

---

### SCREEN 3 — MODULE VIEW (AI Training Space)
The core training screen. IMPORTANT: The AI-generated content is a 
backend feature that cannot be prototyped. Design a beautiful, clear 
placeholder that communicates the intent.

Layout: centered single-column content area with max-width (~760px) 
centered in the main panel, to ensure readability. Sidebar remains 
visible.

Must include:
- Module title and a horizontal step progress bar at the top 
  (e.g., "Module 1: Excel Basics — Step 2 of 5")
- A large, clearly defined CONTENT AREA taking up most of the vertical 
  space. Style it as a modern lesson card or chat-style interface. 
  Inside, add a non-intrusive placeholder: light gray italic text or a 
  dashed border with the label "AI-generated training content will 
  appear here". The area should feel intentional, not empty.
- Below the content area: two action buttons side by side — 
  "I understood, continue" (primary, right-aligned) and 
  "I need help" (secondary, left-aligned)
- A fixed bottom bar or sticky footer with back/forward navigation 
  arrows and a "Ask your tutor" shortcut button
- Clean, distraction-free feel — minimal chrome around the content area

---

### SCREEN 4 — EVALUATION / CHECKPOINT
Shown after completing a module.

Layout: centered content area (~800px max-width), clean and focused.

Must include:
- A clear header: "Module Checkpoint" with the module name as subtitle
- 3–4 placeholder multiple choice questions with realistic dummy content 
  (e.g., "Which Excel function is used to sum a range of cells?")
- Each question is a card with 4 answer options displayed in a 2x2 grid. 
  Selected state shows a highlighted border and background fill.
- A sticky bottom bar with "Submit answers" CTA and an 
  "I'm not ready yet" secondary link
- POST-SUBMISSION STATE: replace the questions with a results card 
  showing score (e.g., "3/4 correct"), a pass/fail indicator with 
  color coding, a motivational message if passed ("Great job! Moving to 
  Module 2"), and a "Retry" button if failed

---

### SCREEN 5 — PROGRESS & ACHIEVEMENTS
A motivational tracker screen.

Layout: three-column grid for the main content area.

Must include:
- Top: a wide stats bar across the full content area — 
  Total time trained | Modules completed | Evaluations passed
- Left column: a visual timeline or progress map of all modules with 
  their completion status, vertically stacked
- Center column: "Achievements" section with badge cards in a grid. 
  Earned badges are colored, unearned are grayscale. Examples: 
  "First module completed", "3-day streak", "First evaluation passed"
- Right column: a "Certificate" card — locked until all modules are 
  complete, showing a certificate icon and the message 
  "Complete all modules to unlock your certificate"

---

### SCREEN 6 — TUTOR CHANNEL (Candidate's view)
Lightweight messaging between candidate and tutor.

Layout: two-panel — tutor info panel on the left (~30%), chat area 
on the right (~70%).

Must include:
- Left panel: tutor profile card with name, title, availability status, 
  and a brief bio placeholder. Below, a small list of recent topics or 
  pinned resources the tutor has shared.
- Right panel: a clean chat interface. Tutor messages appear on the left 
  with avatar, candidate replies on the right. Visual distinction between 
  the two. Tutor messages can include "nudges" like 
  "I see you're stuck on Module 2 — here's a tip: [message]"
- Fixed input bar at the bottom of the chat panel with a text field 
  and send button
- A subtle timestamp below each message group

---

### SCREEN 7 — TUTOR DASHBOARD (Tutor's view)
The tutor's main view of all assigned candidates.

Layout: full use of desktop space — sidebar + top bar shell, main area 
as a data-rich panel.

Must include:
- An ALERT BANNER at the very top of the content area for urgent cases 
  (e.g., "2 candidates have been inactive for 3+ days — Review now")
- A filter/search bar row: search by name, filter by status 
  (All / On track / Needs attention / Inactive)
- A candidate TABLE or card-grid below, showing for each candidate: 
  Name | Job they're training for | Overall progress bar | 
  Last activity | Status badge
- Each row/card is clickable and leads to Screen 8
- The table should feel data-rich but not overwhelming — use generous 
  row height, clear typography, and color-coded status badges

---

### SCREEN 8 — CANDIDATE DETAIL (Tutor's view)
Deep dive on a single candidate.

Layout: two-column — candidate summary on the left (~35%), 
detailed panels on the right (~65%).

Must include:
- Left column: candidate profile card (name, age placeholder, 
  neighborhood, applied job, enrollment date) and a compact overall 
  progress ring. Below: a private "Tutor Notes" section with a 
  text area for internal notes.
- Right column (tabbed or stacked panels):
  - Training progress: module-by-module breakdown with status and 
    progress bars
  - Evaluation history: pass/fail per module with score
  - Activity timeline: chronological feed of actions 
    ("Completed Module 1", "Failed evaluation for Module 2", 
    "Last login: 2 days ago")
- A prominent "Intervene — Open Chat" button fixed at the top right 
  of the right column, leading to Screen 6

---

## IMPORTANT NOTES FOR THE AI

- Do NOT add fake logos, brand names, or real company references
- Use realistic placeholder data (fake names, realistic job titles, 
  realistic module names related to office or administrative work)
- The design must feel like a single coherent web application, 
  not 8 disconnected pages
- Prioritize visual consistency and a unified design language 
  across all screens
- Make full use of horizontal space in every screen — 
  no single-column stretched-mobile layouts
- The AI Training content area (Screen 3) must be clearly treated 
  as a placeholder — do not simulate AI responses
- Use Tailwind CSS for styling if building in React/Next.js
- Deliver all screens within the same persistent layout shell 
  (sidebar + topbar) so they feel like a real web app
- All screens should be visible in a single deliverable, 
  clearly labeled and separated
